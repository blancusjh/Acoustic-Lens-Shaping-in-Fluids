\section{Continuous source synthesis and placement design}
\label{sec:placement}

Let $g(\bm y)$ be a complex normal source-velocity density on the admitted
emitting surface $\Gamma_s$. For the complete chamber Green responses,
\begin{equation}
 P_\ell(\bm x)=\int_{\Gamma_s}G_{p\ell}(\bm x,\bm y)g(\bm y)\dd A_y,
 \qquad
 \bm V_\ell(\bm x)=\int_{\Gamma_s}\bm G_{v\ell}(\bm x,\bm y)g(\bm y)\dd A_y.
 \label{eq:source-green-integrals}
\end{equation}
The responses include all transmission, wall, impedance and geometry effects;
a free-space Green function is generally not interchangeable with them.
Substitution in the radiation tensor gives the central continuous inverse
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_\Gamma[g](\bm x)=
 \int_{\Gamma_s}\int_{\Gamma_s}g(\bm y)^*
 K_\Gamma(\bm x;\bm y,\bm y')g(\bm y')\dd A_y\dd A_{y'}
 =\Pi_{\rm req}(\bm x).
 \label{eq:continuous-load-integral}
\end{equation}
There is one equation on each controlled face and one common source field.
The Hermitian kernel is the pressure/velocity bilinear version of
\cref{eq:quadratic-load}. Its sign, attainable range and bounded-effort
limitations remain those of \cref{sec:reachability}.

Finite emitters give $g_N=\sum_k a_k b_k(\bm y;\bm r_k,\ell_k)$, with
support size $\ell_k$ and positions $\bm r_k=(R_s,\phi_k,z_k)$ on a cylinder.
An axisymmetric restriction uses rings, but a ring-only control cannot in
general correct non-axisymmetric disturbances. Position, support, frequency
and commands can be jointly designed. If $e_i=\bm a^\dagger K_i\bm a-b_i$
and $J=\tfrac12\sum_i(e_i/F_{i,0})^2$, then at fixed target
\begin{align}
 D_aJ[\delta a]&=2\Rea\sum_i\frac{e_i}{F_{i,0}^2}
                        (K_i\bm a)^\dagger\delta\bm a,\\
 \partial_{r_{k,\alpha}}J&=\sum_i\frac{e_i}{F_{i,0}^2}
              \bm a^\dagger(\partial_{r_{k,\alpha}}K_i)\bm a.
 \label{eq:position-gradient}
\end{align}
Constraint derivatives and source-effort penalties must be added. If changing
placement also changes a reflecting or absorbing boundary, the derivative of
the wave operator contributes; differentiating the source term alone misses it.
Frequency derivatives likewise include material dispersion and impedances.

Point controls may be written formally as measures
$\mu=\sum_k a_k\delta_{\bm r_k}$ with a total-variation penalty, following
measure formulations of Helmholtz inversion \cite{pieper2020}. Their units,
singular near fields and relation to finite emitter patches must be declared.
Linear inverse-source recovery does not establish nonlinear traction synthesis.
Source separation and control-region geometry materially affect the effort
and conditioning of active field synthesis \cite{hubenthal2016,egarguin2018}.

Source refinement and field refinement are separate experiments. First freeze
the actual source support, frequency and complex physical command function
and refine the forward field and surface representation. Then compare nested
source spaces under the same pointwise velocity and power bounds, each with
its own independently checked forward solution. More source coefficients
cannot compensate for an unresolved wave discretization. An optimized coarse
field may select precisely the errors that vanish under refinement.
