\large Geometric constraints, dual traction, distributed sources and stability\\
with Cartesian ovoids and simultaneous front--back shaping}
\author{Acoustic Freeform Lab\\\small Private research manuscript}
\date{23 September 2026 \quad---\quad Version 3.9}

\begin{abstract}
We organize acoustic lens shaping as a sequence from prescribed geometry to
required traction, continuous acoustic synthesis, finite source realization,
and maintained operation. The multiplier field enforcing the target surface is
its required normal traction. Extending this variational inverse to both lens
faces exposes their shared volume, pressure and acoustic coupling. A continuous
quadratic integral operator maps distributed sources to loads; array placement,
frequency and amplitudes restrict and optimize that map. A local analytic
pressure-node construction illustrates conditional realizability and supplies
a necessary carrier-displacement bound. Cartesian ovoids specialize the general
geometry without defining the actuator architecture. The accuracy specification
is a maximum absolute cycle-mean error of ten nanometres on each driven-liquid
face; instantaneous motion is a separate diagnostic. Reachability, formation from the
unforced equilibrium, and stability under three-dimensional disturbances remain
distinct conditions. The results are conditional mathematical deductions and a
verification framework; they do not establish ten-nanometre performance of a
physical apparatus.
\end{abstract}
