\section{Initial simultaneous two-face numerical evidence}
\label{sec:dual-evidence}
The accompanying repository contains an axisymmetric restriction of the
three-phase transmission problem with nonlinear capillarity and gravity.
The hypothetical liquid densities are $(1200,1100,1000)\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,
sound speeds $(1500,1200,1500)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and both interface tensions
are $0.025\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$. The cylinder radius is $4\,\mathrm{mm}$;
both clear radii are $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Two volume-preserving, rim-pinned graph
spaces describe interfaces nominally at $z=\pm1\,\mathrm{mm}$.
All source ports act through one pressure field. Thus these are not two
uncoupled one-interface fits. The materials and source impedances are
exploratory assumptions, not measured apparatus parameters.

The first Cartesian pair uses front parameters
$(n_o,z_o,n_i,z_i)=(1.33,-0.1\,\mathrm m,1.5,0.2\,\mathrm m)$ and back parameters
$(1.5,-0.15\,\mathrm m,1.33,0.15\,\mathrm m)$, with vertex displacements
$-150\,\mu\mathrm m$ and $+120\,\mu\mathrm m$ from their nominal planes.
These prescribe independent geometric patches, not a demonstrated stigmatic
two-surface optical system. The outside annuli satisfy the respective volume
constraints. Results are absolute maximum mean-height errors, without fitting
away rigid offsets:

These values are independently recomputed stationary equilibria on each
candidate's refined grid, holding the physical source commands fixed.
The 112-port candidate changes from approximately $(292,97)$ nm on its design
grid to $(284,97)$ nm after refinement. Its sampled carrier-height amplitudes
are approximately $(255,62)$ nm, with acoustic source work approximately
$32.1\,\mathrm W$ and sampled peak pressure $9.47\,\mathrm{MPa}$ in the
ideal source-port model. These are not calibrated electrical drive requirements
or experimentally validated operating pressures. Neither the mean nor the instantaneous
ten-nanometre requirement is met. Changing frequency also changed numerical
resolution and flux recovery; the table is not a controlled isolation of a
frequency effect. All four optimizations stopped at their evaluation limits,
so no globally optimal residual or physical unreachability bound is inferred.

Independent verification compares the pressure and recovered normal velocity
with six-amplitude analytic three-layer scattering, including absolute source
work, and compares capillary compliance with a volume-preserving Bessel mode.
These tests do not validate streaming, formation, thermal feedback or
three-dimensional stability: those effects are absent from this numerical
restriction. Two-grid comparisons are sensitivity checks, not a certified
nanometre discretization budget. Full configurations, executed-source snapshots,
states, failed criteria and further source/placement trials are retained in
the repository's dated dual-Cartesian campaign; its numerical findings document
is the detailed evidence index. No experimental validation is claimed.

\paragraph{Precision-screening warning (23 September 2026).}
A subsequent 224-region, 3.6 MHz frozen-compliance candidate gives front/back
surrogate maxima of approximately $(13.14,18.38)$ nm. A fresh stationary
solution on its design grid instead gives mean errors $(382.71,135.30)$ nm;
its fixed-command refinement was interrupted without a fine-grid equilibrium.
This observed discrepancy directly demonstrates that a small capillary-only
surrogate is not a coupled accuracy certificate. A 448-region, 7.2 MHz
screen reduces the surrogate to $(1.84,2.05)$ nm but retains carrier peaks
$(9.93,11.73)$ nm. It therefore fails the conservative combined screening
criterion used before the user selected cycle-mean accuracy. Its coupled mean
check is still pending, so no mean-surface pass is established. These trials changed
frequency and discretization as well as the number of source regions, and
their optimizations exhausted their budgets. No emitter-count scaling law,
global optimum, or achieved ten-nanometre precision follows. Regions are
ideal full-azimuth annuli and wall bands, not calibrated point emitters.
